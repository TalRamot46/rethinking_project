READING THIS TABLE

The direct leave-one-out peak sits on the posterior mode of the model
that estimated sigma with a dexp(1) prior. That is the result: a purely
predictive criterion and a purely Bayesian one pick the same amount of
regularization, and the multilevel model found it without being told.
The two are not required to agree exactly -- they optimise related but
different objectives, and the dexp(1) prior pulls the posterior mode
slightly down -- so the claim is agreement, not identity. The grid is
also coarse, and resolves sigma only to the nearest grid point.

PSIS and WAIC peak at noticeably LARGER sigma, and they should not be
believed here. Every listing has exactly one binomial observation, so
dropping listing i removes all information about a[i]. Both criteria
approximate that using a posterior in which a[i] was fitted to y[i], so
they undercount the cost of flexibility and drift toward weaker
pooling. The bad_k column is the direct evidence: the number of points
whose Pareto k exceeds 0.7, out of 200 .

The direct computation does no importance sampling. It handles a[i]
exactly -- dropping listing i leaves a[i] with only its prior, which is
integrated analytically in the formula above. Its one approximation is
that it reuses the full-data posterior of a_bar instead of refitting
without listing i, which shifts a_bar by a fraction of its own
posterior sd when one listing in 200 is removed. That is a far milder
approximation than the one PSIS is making, and it is not the
approximation that pushes PSIS and WAIC toward larger sigma.

loco_check repeats the direct computation with independent random
draws and a tenth of the sample, to confirm the peak is not Monte
Carlo noise.
